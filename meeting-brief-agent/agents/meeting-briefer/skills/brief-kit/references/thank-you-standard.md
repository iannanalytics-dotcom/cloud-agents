# Thank-you standard

The owner sends it themselves after the meeting: n8n emails them the draft with an "Open in Outlook" button, and they
read it, change anything they like and press Send. Write it as the owner, in the first person, the way they write:
warm, plain Australian English, short sentences.

## Shape: 70 to 150 words
- **Subject:** short and personal, for example "Thank you for the coffee, Sam" or "Lovely to meet you, Priya".
- **Greeting:** "Hi <first name>," on its own line.
- **Thanks + one specific thing** from the meeting notes (what they talked about, something the owner enjoyed hearing).
- **What the owner promised**, in their words from the notes: introductions, a resource, a follow-up. Add a time frame
  only if the notes give one. Never add a promise that is not in the notes.
- **One warm touch, only if the notes have it**: something they shared (a new apprentice, a milestone). One sentence.
- **Next step** from the notes (catch up again in a month, see you at the next group meeting).
- **Sign-off:** "Warm regards," then YOUR_OWNER_FIRST_NAME on its own line. No signature block, phone, email or links:
  the owner's mail app adds their signature.

## Never
- Pitch the owner's business, its services or prices, or mention the brief, research or an agent.
- Invent anything that is not in the notes, or repeat anything the notes mark as private.
- Use em or en dashes, emojis, exclamation-mark strings, or salesy words ("excited to partner", "synergy").

## Example (made up, for the format only)
Subject: Lovely to meet you, Priya
```
Hi Priya,

Thank you for the coffee near the station this morning. I really enjoyed hearing how Northgate started and why you
chose to work with cafes and trades.

As promised, I will introduce you to the cafe owner I mentioned before the end of the week, and I have noted that you
would like to meet a commercial cleaner.

I hope your first month of 1-2-1s keeps going this well. See you at the next group meeting.

Warm regards,
Jordan
```
